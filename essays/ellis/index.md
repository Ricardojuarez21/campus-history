The James & Gail Ellis School of Business Leadership at the University of New Mexico is part of a larger story about how a university prepares students to lead in business, serve their communities, and shape New Mexico’s economy. Located in the McKinnon Center, the school connects business education with the physical spaces where students learn, collaborate, and prepare for their careers. Looking at the school’s history raises important questions: How has business education at UNM changed over time, and what do the school’s building, leadership, and institutional history reveal about the university’s priorities?


## Main Story

The history of the James & Gail Ellis School of Business Leadership is connected to the development of business education at the University of New Mexico. UNM has served students throughout New Mexico and the Southwest since its founding in 1889. As the university expanded, professional education became an important part of its mission, preparing students for careers in fields that included management, finance, accounting, and entrepreneurship.
The university’s Anderson School of Management provides the broader academic context for understanding business education at UNM. Within this environment, leadership education emphasizes more than learning how businesses operate. It also involves decision-making, communication, teamwork, and understanding the responsibilities that business leaders have toward employees and communities.
The James & Gail Ellis School of Business Leadership, located in the McKinnon Center, represents a more specific focus on leadership development. Its name recognizes individuals associated with the school, while its location provides a physical setting for students to engage with business education. To establish the school's founding date, the circumstances behind its naming, and the contributions of James and Gail Ellis, archival records and official university documents are important sources of evidence.
One of the most important moments in this history occurred on May 8, 2026, when UNM announced a $30 million commitment from James “Jim” Ellis and Gail Ellis. The gift led to the renaming of the Anderson School of Management as the James & Gail Ellis School of Business Leadership. According to the university, it was the first time a UNM college or school had been named in recognition of a philanthropic investment. The announcement marked a major change in the school's identity and signaled a commitment to expanding opportunities for future business students. Jim Ellis's connection to UNM gives the gift a personal dimension. He earned his Bachelor of Business Administration from the university in 1968 and later received an MBA from Harvard University. His professional career included twelve years as dean of the University of Southern California's Marshall School of Business. His experience in business education connects UNM's local mission with a broader conversation about how universities prepare future business leaders.

## What Changed

The McKinnon Center opened in May 2018, after an 18-month construction project. The new facility provided 63,445 square feet of space, including classrooms, technology services, a career planning and placement center, advising areas, a behavioral research laboratory, and a marketing center. UNM described the project as a major milestone for the business school.The building was made possible in part by a $7.5 million commitment from Sonnet and Ian McKinnon in 2018. Their gift supported three UNM programs: the business school, University Arena (known as The Pit), and the UNM Tennis Complex. The McKinnon Center was designed with student-focused services, collaborative study areas, and sustainability in mind, including standards associated with LEED Gold certification. These details show how philanthropy can influence not only a university's budget but also the spaces available for teaching and learning. The 2026 renaming added another layer to this history. The school had educated business students under the Anderson name since 1947, and that legacy did not disappear when the new name was announced. Instead, the Ellis name became the latest chapter in an institution that has changed through generations of students, faculty, donors, and administrators. The new commitment also emphasized scholarships and financial support, experiential learning, faculty development, and a curriculum responsive to changes in the economy. This history illustrates a larger pattern at UNM: major gifts can shape institutional priorities, support new facilities, and change how a university presents itself to the public. Such investments can create opportunities, but they also raise questions worth investigating. How are new resources distributed among students and programs? How will the university measure the gift's long-term effects? And how will it balance donor recognition with the school's older institutional identity?

## Evidence

The university's 2018 announcement about the McKinnon Center is one useful starting point for examining the building's history. It records the opening date, the length of construction, the facility's size, and the services included in the project. These details help establish what the university intended the building to accomplish when it opened. The announcement also presents the center as a milestone, revealing how administrators described the importance of investment in business education at that time. A second important source is UNM's announcement of the Ellis family's 2026 gift. It documents the amount of the commitment, the reason for the new name, and the university's stated goals for the investment. Read alongside the 2018 announcement, it shows how the business school's public story shifted from developing a new physical space to investing in the institution's future and expanding student opportunities. These public announcements are useful evidence, but they have limitations. They explain the university's official account of its investments and aspirations; they do not, by themselves, establish how every student experienced the changes or whether the promised benefits have been fully realized. They also do not replace original correspondence, construction plans, financial records, or meeting minutes.
For an archival history, the next step would be to consult UNM's Center for Southwest Research for records related to the Anderson School of Management, the McKinnon Center project, and university fundraising. Original documents could help answer questions about how the building was planned, how decisions were made, and how students and faculty responded. ## Why This History Matters

Today, the McKinnon Center remains an important part of the student experience at UNM's James & Gail Ellis School of Business Leadership. Its classrooms, advising services, and collaborative spaces support students as they prepare for careers in business and other professional fields. The building also preserves the legacy of the McKinnon family's investment, even as the school adopts a new name and a new philanthropic commitment.
The change from Anderson to Ellis reveals how campus history develops through both continuity and change. The school retains a legacy of business education dating to 1947, while the 2018 building project and the 2026 gift represent two distinct moments when financial support helped shape its future. One strengthened the physical environment for learning; the other created an opportunity to expand educational resources and redefine the school's public identity.
Understanding this history means looking beyond a building's name or a donor's contribution. It means asking how these investments affect students, what institutional values they express, and whose experiences are preserved in the university's historical record. The McKinnon Center is more than a campus building: it is a place where the history of philanthropy, education, and New Mexico's economic future continues to unfold.
